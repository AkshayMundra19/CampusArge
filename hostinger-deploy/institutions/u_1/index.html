Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT)

Private research university in Cambridge, Massachusetts, founded in 1861, world-renowned for science, engineering, and technology.

MIT's mission is to advance knowledge and educate students in science, technology, and other areas of scholarship that will best serve the world. It is organized around five Schools plus the Schwarzman College of Computing and is known for a hands-on, problem-solving culture ('mens et manus').

Key Institutional Facts

  • Cost of Attendance: $77,020 / year
  • Student-to-Faculty Ratio: 3:1
  • Classification: Private Research University
  • Accreditation: NECHE Accredited
  • Undergraduate Students: 4,530 undergraduates
  • Campus Size: 168 acres
  • Housing Capacity: ~4,800 beds
  • Average Rent: $3,500 / month

Research Focus

Artificial intelligence and computing (CSAIL), robotics, energy and climate, biotechnology and life sciences, materials science, aerospace, nuclear science, economics, and the Media Lab for digital technology and design.

Academic Programs

Engineering, Science, Architecture and Planning, Humanities Arts and Social Sciences, Sloan School of Management, Computing (Schwarzman College of Computing)
